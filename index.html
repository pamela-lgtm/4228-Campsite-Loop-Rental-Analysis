Section Two

The calendar is the biggest single factor

You asked us to look at when these homes came to market, not just what they leased for. That turned out to be the most important thing in the data.

Average days to lease by listing month. The October figure is a single home, but it is the only comparable in this community that launched in the fall.

Homes that came to market in April, May and June leased in about a month — the spring and early-summer window when families relocate and the rental market is at its most active.

Then it changes. The one home listed in July took 68 days. And 4410 Campsite Loop, listed in late October 2025, took 287 days — more than nine months — and had to cut its price $150 along the way. It finally leased in August 2026, once the market came back around.

We are listing at the end of September. That is the beginning of the slower half of the year, not the end of the busy one. It does not mean the home won't lease — it means the price has to do more of the work than it would have in May.

The current listings confirm it. Five homes are on the market now, all listed between late July and mid-September. Not one of them has leased. Two have been sitting 51 and 57 days.

Section Five

Why $2,395

It clears the $2,400 search threshold

Renters browse in price brackets, and $2,400 is a common ceiling. Every competing listing sits above it. At $2,395 your home appears in searches none of them reaches, and is the least expensive townhome available in Tyson Ranch.

It reflects the season, not just the size

The band for a home your size is $2,400 to $2,500. Spring launches achieved the top of it; the July launch achieved $2,400 only after a $100 reduction and 68 days. Launching just under the bottom of that band, in late September, is how we avoid repeating that.

It separates you from the two listings at $2,450

$55 is enough for a renter to notice in search results, and it puts you in a different bracket from both. Against the larger home at the same price, being the lower number is the argument that works.

It pays for itself quickly

Launching at $2,395 rather than $2,450 costs $55 a month, or $660 across a 12-month lease. Every vacant day costs about $80. The lower price pays for itself if it leases just eight days sooner — and the home at $2,450 is already 51 days in.

Section Six

How we'll work with the market

We will not promise a number of days. In a market where identical homes took 13 days in April and 68 days in July, any promise would be invented. What we will do is measure and react.

The first five days are the signal. We track inquiries, showings booked and showings actually attended. Early activity tells us whether the price is landing before the calendar costs us anything.

If that activity isn't strong enough for us to choose a well-qualified resident rather than accept whoever applies, we move to $2,345. If the market says that is still not enough, we go to $2,295 and keep reading it. $2,295 is a step, not a bottom.

We also watch the five competing listings daily. Three of them are priced far above the market and will have to reduce. When they do, the field moves and we need to move with it — not three weeks later.

The pattern in this community is consistent: homes that price correctly at launch lease at their asking price. Homes that test a higher number reduce anyway, and pay for the test in weeks of vacancy. Every vacant week costs about $560.
